About Us

Why we believe every African dish deserves a seat at the world’s table.

Our mission

Tastes of Africa exists to celebrate, preserve and share the continent’s food heritage — so that the recipes our grandmothers cooked by heart are known, cooked and loved everywhere.

A brief history of African cuisine

Africa is the birthplace of some of the world’s oldest crops. Sorghum, millet, teff, African rice, yams, okra, black-eyed peas and coffee were all domesticated here thousands of years ago.

Trade reshaped the pantry. Trans-Saharan caravans and Indian Ocean dhows carried spices, rice and citrus; from the 1500s, crops from the Americas — maize, cassava, tomatoes, chillies and peanuts — arrived and were woven so deeply into local cooking that they now feel native.

Global influence

African cooks carried their knowledge across the world. Enslaved West Africans brought okra, rice-growing skills and one-pot stews to the Americas, giving rise to gumbo, jambalaya, Brazilian acarajé and Caribbean rice and peas.

Today the influence continues: coffee from Ethiopia fuels the planet, Moroccan tagines and Ethiopian injera are served in every major city, and a new generation of chefs is bringing African flavours to the world’s best restaurants.

Visit a food landmark

Jemaa el-Fnaa, Marrakech. Every evening this historic square fills with hundreds of open-air food stalls, grills and storytellers. It is recognised by UNESCO as a masterpiece of living heritage.
