Bunny Chow

Durban street food: a hollowed loaf filled with fiery Durban curry.

  • Origin: South Africa
  • Region: Southern Africa
  • Time: 1 hr 20 min
  • Serves: 4
  • Level: Medium

The story behind the dish

Bunny chow was born in Durban, home to one of the largest Indian communities outside India. Indentured labourers and traders brought their curries to KwaZulu-Natal in the 19th century, and the hollowed-out bread loaf became an edible lunchbox.

There is no rabbit involved: the name is often linked to the Bania merchants who sold it. A “quarter mutton” is the classic order, eaten with your hands, using the scooped-out bread to mop up the gravy.

Cooking instructions

Click a step to expand or collapse it.

Step 1: Start the curry
  1. Heat the oil and fry the cinnamon, cardamom and curry leaves until fragrant.
  2. Add the onions and cook until golden, then stir in the ginger-garlic paste and masala.
Step 2: Simmer
  1. Brown the lamb in the spices, then add the tomatoes and salt.
  2. Add a cup of water, cover and simmer for 40 minutes.
  3. Add the potatoes and cook for another 20 minutes until soft and the gravy is thick.
Step 3: Fill the bunny
  1. Cut the loaf into quarters and hollow out each piece, keeping the bread you remove.
  2. Ladle in the hot curry, top with coriander and place the bread “lid” on top.

Ingredients

Servings 4
  • 600 g lamb or mutton, cubed
  • 1 unsliced white bread loaf
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 3 tomatoes, chopped
  • 3 potatoes, cubed
  • 3 tbsp Durban masala (curry powder)
  • 1 tbsp ginger-garlic paste
  • 1 cinnamon stick and 2 cardamom pods
  • A sprig of curry leaves
  • 3 tbsp oil
  • Fresh coriander
  • Salt to taste
