Recipes / Ethiopia

Injera

Spongy, tangy sourdough flatbread made from ancient teff grain.

  • Origin: Ethiopia
  • Region: East Africa
  • Time: 3 days (fermenting)
  • Serves: 8
  • Level: Advanced

The story behind the dish

Injera is the plate, the cutlery and the heart of every Ethiopian and Eritrean meal. It is made from teff, a tiny iron-rich grain cultivated in the Horn of Africa for thousands of years, fermented for days to develop its gentle sourness.

Stews called wot are spooned onto one large shared injera. Diners tear pieces with the right hand to scoop up the food, and offering a hand-fed bite — a gursha — is a gesture of love and respect.

Cooking instructions

Click a step to expand or collapse it.

Step 1: Ferment the batter
  1. Whisk the teff flour, water and starter into a smooth batter.
  2. Cover loosely and leave at room temperature for 2–3 days until bubbly and sour.
Step 2: Prepare the absit
  1. Pour off any dark liquid from the top of the batter.
  2. Cook ½ cup of batter with 1 cup of water until thick, cool it, and stir it back in with the salt.
  3. Rest for 1–2 hours until the batter rises again.
Step 3: Cook the injera
  1. Heat a lightly oiled non-stick pan or mitad over medium heat.
  2. Pour the batter in a spiral from the outside in to make a thin layer.
  3. When holes (“eyes”) form, cover and steam for 2 minutes. Do not flip. Cool on a cloth.

Ingredients

Servings 8
  • 2 cups teff flour
  • 3 cups lukewarm water
  • ¼ cup ersho (starter) or ¼ tsp dry yeast
  • ½ tsp salt
  • Neutral oil for the pan
