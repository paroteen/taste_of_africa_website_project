Isombe

Rwanda’s comforting stew of pounded cassava leaves and peanut.

  • Origin: Rwanda
  • Region: East Africa
  • Time: 1 hr 30 min
  • Serves: 6
  • Level: Easy

The story behind the dish

Isombe is a beloved Rwandan dish made from cassava leaves pounded in a wooden mortar until fine, then simmered slowly until silky. Cassava is one of the Great Lakes region’s most important crops, and nothing is wasted: roots become flour and ubugari, leaves become isombe.

Enriched with ground peanuts and palm oil, it is served at family gatherings and weddings alongside rice, plantains (ibitoke) or ubugari. Similar dishes — sombe, pondu, saka-saka — are shared across Burundi and the Congo.

Cooking instructions

Click a step to expand or collapse it.

Step 1: Boil the leaves
  1. Place the pounded cassava leaves in a large pot and cover with water.
  2. Boil for 45 minutes, stirring now and then, until the leaves are tender and no longer bitter.
Step 2: Add the vegetables
  1. Add the onions, leeks, pepper, aubergines and garlic.
  2. Simmer for a further 20 minutes until the vegetables are soft.
Step 3: Enrich and serve
  1. Stir in the palm oil and the ground peanuts loosened with a little warm water.
  2. Cook on low heat for 15 minutes until thick and creamy, then season with salt.
  3. Serve hot with rice, plantains or ubugari.

Ingredients

  • 500 g pounded cassava leaves (fresh or frozen)
  • 1 cup ground peanuts or peanut butter
  • 2 onions, chopped
  • 2 leeks or spring onions
  • 1 green bell pepper
  • 2 small aubergines (intoryi), chopped
  • 3 tbsp palm oil
  • 2 cloves garlic
  • Salt to taste
  • Optional: dried fish or beef
