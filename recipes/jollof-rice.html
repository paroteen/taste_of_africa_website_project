Jollof Rice

Smoky, tomato-rich party rice simmered with scotch bonnet and thyme.

  • Origin: Nigeria
  • Region: West Africa
  • Time: 1 hr 10 min
  • Serves: 6
  • Level: Medium

The story behind the dish

No West African celebration is complete without a steaming pot of jollof. The dish traces its roots to the Wolof (Jolof) Empire of the Senegambia region, where rice was cooked in a rich tomato and fish base known today as thieboudienne.

As it travelled along trade routes, each country made it its own. Nigerians prize the smoky “party jollof” cooked over firewood, while Ghanaians favour fragrant basmati — a friendly rivalry known across the continent as the Jollof Wars.

Cooking instructions

Click a step to expand or collapse it.

Step 1: Make the pepper base
  1. Blend the tomatoes, bell peppers, scotch bonnet and one onion until smooth.
  2. Boil the blend in a pot for 10–15 minutes until most of the water evaporates.
Step 2: Fry the stew
  1. Heat the oil and fry the remaining sliced onion until golden.
  2. Add the tomato paste and fry for 5 minutes, stirring so it does not burn.
  3. Pour in the reduced pepper base, curry, thyme and bay leaves; fry for 10 minutes.
Step 3: Steam the rice
  1. Add the stock and season to taste, then bring to a boil.
  2. Stir in the washed rice, cover tightly with foil and a lid, and cook on low heat for 30 minutes.
  3. Let the bottom catch slightly for the signature smoky flavour, then fluff and serve with fried plantain.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups long-grain parboiled rice
  • 5 ripe plum tomatoes
  • 2 red bell peppers (tatashe)
  • 1–2 scotch bonnet peppers
  • 2 medium onions
  • ¼ cup vegetable oil
  • 3 tbsp tomato paste
  • 3 cups chicken or vegetable stock
  • 2 tsp curry powder
  • 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 2 bay leaves
  • Salt and seasoning cubes to taste
