Chicken Tagine

Slow-braised chicken with preserved lemon, olives and saffron.

  • Origin: Morocco
  • Region: North Africa
  • Time: 1 hr 30 min
  • Serves: 4
  • Level: Easy

The story behind the dish

A tagine is both the dish and the cone-lidded clay pot it is cooked in. The shape is a piece of Amazigh (Berber) engineering: steam rises, condenses on the cool cone and drips back down, so meat turns tender with very little water — ideal for an arid land.

Moroccan tagines balance sweet, sour and savoury, layering ras el hanout, saffron and ginger with preserved lemons, olives, dates or apricots. It is traditionally eaten communally, scooped up with pieces of khobz bread.

Cooking instructions

Click a step to expand or collapse it.

Step 1: Marinate the chicken
  1. Mix the garlic, ginger, turmeric, ras el hanout, saffron and olive oil.
  2. Rub the mixture over the chicken and rest for at least 30 minutes.
Step 2: Build the tagine
  1. Layer the sliced onions in the base of a tagine or heavy pot.
  2. Arrange the chicken on top and pour in the water or stock.
  3. Cover and simmer gently on low heat for 45 minutes.
Step 3: Finish and serve
  1. Add the preserved lemon and olives, then cook uncovered for 15 minutes until the sauce thickens.
  2. Scatter with chopped coriander and parsley and serve with warm bread or couscous.

Ingredients

Servings 4
  • 8 bone-in chicken thighs
  • 2 onions, finely sliced
  • 3 cloves garlic, crushed
  • 1 preserved lemon, quartered
  • 1 cup green olives
  • 1 tsp ground ginger
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1 tsp ras el hanout
  • A pinch of saffron threads
  • 3 tbsp olive oil
  • 1 cup water or stock
  • Fresh coriander and parsley
