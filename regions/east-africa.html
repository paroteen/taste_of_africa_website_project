Regions / East Africa

East Africa

From the Ethiopian highlands to the Great Lakes and the Swahili coast, East African food is about sharing from a single plate.

Food culture

In Ethiopia and Eritrea, meals are served on injera and seasoned with berbere, a deep-red blend of chilli, fenugreek and warm spices. Coffee, first discovered here, is honoured in an elaborate roasting and brewing ceremony.

Around the Great Lakes, Rwandans, Ugandans and Kenyans rely on hearty staples — ugali, plantains, beans and cassava — while the Swahili coast shows centuries of Indian Ocean trade in its coconut curries, pilau and chapati.

Staple ingredients

  • Teff, maize meal and sorghum
  • Plantains (matoke) and cassava
  • Beans, lentils and groundnuts
  • Collard greens (sukuma wiki) and cassava leaves
  • Berbere, cardamom, cloves and coconut

Cooking techniques

  • Fermenting teff batter for days to create sour, spongy injera.
  • Nyama choma: slow-roasting goat or beef over charcoal.
  • Pounding cassava leaves in a wooden mortar for isombe.
  • Stirring maize meal into boiling water until it forms firm ugali.
