Regions / North Africa

North Africa

Where the Sahara meets the Mediterranean, the cuisines of the Maghreb and Egypt blend Amazigh, Arab, Andalusian and Ottoman influences.

Food culture

North African cooking is perfumed rather than fiery. Spice merchants blend ras el hanout — literally “head of the shop” — from dozens of spices, and cooks balance savoury meat with sweet dried fruit, honey and preserved lemons.

Hospitality is sacred: guests are welcomed with sweet mint tea poured from a height, and Friday couscous is a family ritual. In 2020, the knowledge and practices of couscous were inscribed on UNESCO’s list of intangible cultural heritage.

Staple ingredients

  • Couscous and semolina breads
  • Lamb, chicken and chickpeas
  • Olives, olive oil and preserved lemons
  • Dates, almonds and apricots
  • Harissa, cumin, saffron and cinnamon

Cooking techniques

  • Slow braising in a cone-lidded clay tagine that recycles steam.
  • Steaming couscous two or three times in a couscoussier above a simmering stew.
  • Preserving lemons in salt for weeks until soft and intensely fragrant.
  • Baking flatbreads in communal neighbourhood wood-fired ovens.
