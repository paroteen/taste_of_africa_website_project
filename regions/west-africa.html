Regions / West Africa

West Africa

From the markets of Lagos to the coast of Dakar, West African cooking is generous, deeply savoury and unafraid of heat.

Food culture

West African food is built on a holy trinity of tomato, onion and chilli, cooked down into the stew bases that flavour everything from jollof rice to egusi soup. Meals are social and plentiful: one large pot, many hands.

Fermented seasonings such as iru (dawadawa) and dried crayfish give dishes a deep umami, while red palm oil adds colour and richness. The region’s cooking travelled across the Atlantic and shaped the cuisines of Brazil, the Caribbean and the American South.

Staple ingredients

  • Rice, yam, cassava and plantain
  • Fufu, banku, eba and other “swallows”
  • Black-eyed peas and groundnuts (peanuts)
  • Scotch bonnet peppers, ginger and grains of selim
  • Smoked and dried fish

Cooking techniques

  • Pounding boiled yam or cassava in a mortar to make smooth, stretchy fufu.
  • Open-fire grilling of suya skewers rubbed with yaji, a peanut-and-chilli spice mix.
  • Slow stewing in one pot so rice absorbs every drop of flavour.
  • Smoking and sun-drying fish to preserve it and deepen its taste.
